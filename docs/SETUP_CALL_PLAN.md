# MaeDay setup call: SPIN plan (20 minutes)

The site's main call to action books this call, so it is a short discovery call. Questions use US wording, since the callers are US property managers.

## Objective

- **Advance:** they pick a plan and pay (order form or annual invoice), and the build starts with their emergency list, properties and on-call ladder agreed on the call.
- **Fallback advance:** a second 15-minute call this week with whoever signs (owner, broker or partner), with the forwardable summary sent the same day.
- Not an advance: "send me some info".

## Who is usually on it

| Role | Cares about |
|---|---|
| Owner or broker (decision-maker) | Cost, liability, owner complaints, not being woken |
| Property manager (user, often the champion) | Their own nights, tenants shouting, owners asking what happened |
| Maintenance lead (influencer) | Being called for things that could wait, fair on-call rotation |

## Before the call (research, so no wasted situation questions)

Website and Google profile: number of properties or doors, residential, HOA or mixed, current after-hours message (call their main line out of hours and note what happens), property software named on the site (AppFolio, Buildium, Rent Manager and so on), states they operate in.

## Opening (2 min)

"Thanks for booking. I'm Andy from MaeDay. The aim today is to understand how your after-hours calls work now and whether Mae would fit. If it does, we'll set your emergency rules and the order your team gets called. Mind if I ask a few questions first?"

## Situation (3 min, confirm only)

- "You manage about [X] doors across [Y] properties, mostly [type]. Is that right?"
- "When the office closes, where do calls go today?"
- "Who's on call, and how do you rotate it?"

## Problem (5 min)

- "What usually happens when a tenant calls at 2am?"
- "How often does an after-hours call turn out not to be an emergency?"
- "Have you had a call that didn't reach anyone? What happened?"
- "Where do leasing calls go after 5pm and at weekends?"
- "If you use an answering service now, what does it get wrong?"
- "How do you show an owner what happened on a night call?"

## Implication chains (6 min, plan these before the call)

1. **Missed emergency:** "When that leak sat until morning, what did it reach? ... What did the repair cost compared with catching it at 2am? ... How did the owner take it?"
2. **Wrong calls waking people:** "How many nights a month is your tech woken for something that could wait? ... What does that do to the rotation? ... Have you lost a tech over on-call?"
3. **No record:** "When an owner or tenant disputes when something was reported, what do you have? ... What happens when the repair clock is in question?"
4. **Leasing after hours:** "How many prospects call after the office closes? ... What happens to them if nobody picks up? ... What is a vacant week worth on a typical unit?"
5. **Current service cost:** "What did last month's answering service bill come to? ... How much of that was hold time or message-taking?"

Capture their numbers in their words. Do not supply numbers for them.

## Need-payoff (3 min, only for needs Mae can meet)

- "If every real emergency reached your on-call tech, and the tap drips waited for morning, what would that change for your team?"
- "How would a timestamped log of every attempt help with owners?"
- "If prospects got the same straight answers at 9pm, where would that show up?"
- "Who else in the business would notice?"

## Showing capability (only against stated needs)

For each explicit need, one benefit sentence: "You said [their words]. Mae [does exactly that] because [the rule or ladder we set]." Then play the sample call or call the demo line together. Do not list features they did not ask about.

## Commit (1 min)

Summarise in their words, check concerns (911, Fair Housing, recording notice, texts after carrier registration), then ask:

"Based on about [N] calls a month, [Plan] at $[price] covers you. Setup is $297, or free on annual. Shall I send the order form now so we can start building your rules this week?"

Fallback: "Who else needs to agree? Can we get 15 minutes with them on [day]? I'll send a one-page summary today that you can forward."

## Likely concerns and how to prevent them

| Concern | Prevent by |
|---|---|
| "What if it gets an emergency wrong?" | Building their own list together on the call. Mae follows their rules. Show the log. |
| "Tenants won't like a robot." | Play the sample call. Mae says she's an AI if asked. The outcome is the tech calling back fast. |
| "Fair Housing risk on leasing calls." | Approved script, same answers for everyone, accommodations to a person, every call recorded. |
| "We use AppFolio/Buildium." | It works alongside any software today with an email summary. Integration is not live yet: say so. |
| Price | Go back to the implication they gave (one missed leak, one lost tech, the current bill). Do not discount. |
